MagPlaySID v1.53

Release date: 2026-11-10

A player for Commodore 64 music that shows how a tune is built. Drop any .sid on it: it plays on an emulated C64, and you see it as a tracker would - its notes, instruments and effects in rows that scroll with the music. Drop a whole folder and it becomes a playlist; keep the tunes you love in playlists of your own. Amiga ProTracker modules play too, and Scream Tracker, FastTracker and Impulse Tracker modules, each shown as its tracker shows it - and so do WAV, FLAC, MP3, OGG and AAC recordings, their notes heard and shown as they play.

What it does

Any tune, played as the C64 plays it, and read back as a score.

💾

Any .sid

PSID and RSID, PAL and NTSC, on the raster or a CIA timer - even tunes written in BASIC. The tune's own 6502 code runs on an emulated C64, and every write reaches the SID on the cycle it was made.

🔊

Digis

Samples streamed from an NMI play as they are written, thousands of times a second - the drums and voices a player that calls a tune once a frame never hears. A digi gets a scope of its own.

🎼

Transcribed as you listen

The tune becomes a score - notes, instruments, filter, patterns and order lists - ready in a moment. Its length is measured on the C64 and agrees with HVSC's for more than four songs in five.

🕹️

Tracker modules

Amiga .mod files play as an Amiga 500 plays them, and .s3m, .xm and .it as their trackers do, each shown as its tracker shows it. A slider, kept for each kind, brings the stereo in toward mono.

🎧

Recordings and notes

WAV, FLAC, MP3, OGG and AAC files play too, and their notes are heard as they play - chords as well as melodies - shown as a piano roll with the chord's name. It hears most on a single instrument.

📜

Rows that scroll

Each voice's rows - note, instrument, effects - go past as they sound. Click a voice to see its instrument: envelope, hard restart, and the wave, pulse and filter programs.

🎹

A piano

Eight octaves along the bottom, each voice's key lit in its colour as loud as its envelope - an arpeggio, a slide or a drum shows as it plays. For a recording, every note heard is lit.

🎛️

2SID and 3SID

Tunes for two or three SID chips play on all of them, each the model its header names, with a scope and score columns for every voice - six or nine voices side by side.

📝

Sheet music, text, MIDI

Export notes saves the song as the rows read it: as sheet music, a PDF with a staff for each voice, as text, or as MIDI, a track for each voice - for modules too.

💿

Save the sound

Export sound saves the song as WAV, FLAC or MP3, made many times faster than it plays, fading out when it loops and leaving out muted voices. Modules are saved in stereo.

📂

Drop and play

Drag a .sid, a module or a recording onto the window, open one, or name it on the command line. MagPlaySID opens where you left it; Space pauses, and the arrow keys change tune.

🎶

Playlists

Drop a folder on the window and it becomes a playlist. Make your own with ♥ Add to playlist, drag tunes to reorder them, search them all from the box at the top, and click a tune's ⓘ for all about it.

↕️

Sort a playlist

Sort by year, title, composer, group, file date, songs, length, recently or most played - or shuffle. Your own order is always there to go back to, and nothing needs measuring first.

🌄

Six meters

Spectrum bars, a wireframe landscape, neon waves, Last Ninja fighting on a hilltop, Dire Guys dancing on a mirror ball, or Outrun - a red convertible down a coast road, from a sunny day to a neon night as the song passes its half - every move on the music. Click the meter for the next.

🔢

Every song

A tune's songs are round buttons: click one or press 1-9. Choose which song plays first, repeat a tune up to ten times or forever, or play every song of it before the next tune.

⏩

Wind on, or jump

Hold fast-forward - or the F key - and the tune runs four times as fast, then faster. Or click the time bar to jump there: a recording or a module at once, a .sid once it is read.

⚙️

Yours to set

The knob at the top right sets the volume: drag or scroll it, click to mute. Big font makes the text a quarter larger, and MagPlaySID can look for a newer version each time it opens.

Get started

  1. Download the package for your system from the latest release. .NET is inside - there is nothing else to install.
  2. Windows: unzip it anywhere and run MagPlaySID.exe. Linux: unpack it and run ./MagPlaySID. Mac: unzip it and move MagPlaySID to Applications.
  3. Drop a .sid, a tracker module or a recording on the window, or choose Open tune... - or drop a whole folder to play it as a playlist, or choose Open folder as playlist in the menu. The High Voltage SID Collection has tens of thousands of .sid tunes.
  4. Click a scope to mute its voice; click a voice's rows to see its instrument; click the meter to change it; click a tune's ⓘ in a playlist to read all about it.
  5. Keep the tunes you like: ♥ Add to playlist while one plays - and their notes: Export notes in the menu, as sheet music or text - or their sound: Export sound, as WAV, FLAC or MP3. Keys: Space pauses, ← and → change tune in a playlist, 1-9, + and - pick a song, and F held fast-forwards. Change your speakers while it plays and the sound follows them.
The Windows exe is not code-signed, so Windows SmartScreen may say it comes from an unknown publisher. Choose More info → Run anyway.

On a Mac, the app is not notarised by Apple yet, so macOS stops it the first time it is opened:

  • macOS 15 Sequoia and later: try to open MagPlaySID once and close the warning. Then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down to the line about MagPlaySID and choose Open Anyway.
  • macOS 14 and earlier: right-click MagPlaySID, choose Open, then Open again.
  • If macOS says MagPlaySID is damaged and can't be opened, it is the mark macOS puts on everything downloaded from the internet, not the app. Remove it in Terminal:
xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/MagPlaySID.app

That tells macOS to stop checking this one app, so only do it for software you trust. Either way it is needed once, not every time.

How it works

The sound is the tune's own; the score is a reading of it, checked against it.

A C64, cycle by cycle

CIA timers, the raster interrupt, NMIs, banked memory and the cycles the VIC takes for sprites and the screen, with a 6510 that makes each memory access on the cycle the real chip does.

A score that plays back

The transcription is played as it is made, and every register it gets wrong is written as the tune wrote it. On average its notes and instruments make 96.5% of a tune's register changes.

An Amiga 500, and PC trackers

ProTracker 2's effects, the CIA's timing, Paula's stereo and the A500's filter; for the PC, envelopes, new note actions and IT's filter. Lengths agree with libopenmpt's to 0.3 s.

A recording's notes

Each moment's sound is searched for the harmonics of every key, the strongest notes taken out one by one. On test pieces it scores 66% for notes and their starts, and 87% on piano - on every core at once.
